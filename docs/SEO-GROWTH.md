# PRIME organic growth and authority plan

Principle: PRIME earns links by publishing data that others want to cite. There is no link buying, no automated posting, no outreach at scale and no private blog networks. Everything below is something a person at PRIME does by hand, in their own voice, where it is relevant.

## What makes PRIME citable

Not a claim of being "better": specific, checkable differences. See "Link-worthy data" in `docs/SEO.md`. In short: opponent-adjusted offense and defense with no preseason priors in 2026, a résumé-based PRIME 25 with no voter input, strength of record and schedule for every team, and a public prediction record that includes the losses (PRIME's walk-forward backtest sits slightly behind closing market lines, and publishing that is what builds trust).

## Legitimate opportunities

- **Team and conference communities.** Team subreddits and fan forums often discuss rankings and schedule strength. Share a team page or a specific finding only when it answers something being asked, disclose that it is your project, and follow each community's self-promotion rules. One useful comment beats ten links.
- **Sports and analytics writers.** Data journalists and college football writers cover "who has the hardest schedule" and "where the polls are wrong". Send a short, personal note with one specific finding and the page that shows it. Offer the underlying numbers; do not mass-email.
- **Newsletters and podcasts.** Pitch analytics-focused college football newsletters and podcasts with a concrete segment (see formats below), not a general request for a mention.
- **Analytics and data communities.** Publish the methodology and, if licensing allows, a small public dataset (weekly ratings CSV) on GitHub with a clear README linking to `/methodology`. Researchers cite datasets.
- **X and other social posts.** Post original findings with the OG card and a link to the canonical page. Prefer one strong post a week over daily posting.
- **Shareable graphics.** Add an export ("copy as image") for The PRIME 25 and team cards so fans can post them with attribution. This is the highest-leverage product change for organic links.
- **Local and school media, blogs.** Team-specific bloggers cite their team's analytics page when it says something new about their team.

## Recurring original-research formats

Each format is driven by data PRIME already publishes, is dated, and links to the pages holding the numbers. Do not auto-generate them.

1. **Overrated and underrated by opponent-adjusted performance**: teams where the PRIME rating rank and the AP or PRIME 25 rank differ most. Distinct because PRIME has both performance and résumé measures.
2. **Who has played the hardest schedule**: SOS and SOR leaders and laggards, and what each does to a record.
3. **Biggest movers after Week N**: from published week-over-week rank change, with the game that caused each move.
4. **Best offenses and defenses after opponent adjustment**: leaders and the teams whose raw stats overstate them.
5. **PRIME 25 vs the AP Poll**: the disagreements and the reasoning, published every Sunday after the release.
6. **Prediction report card**: monthly recap of graded picks, including misses and the market comparison.

## What to track

Referring domains and the pages they link to (Search Console Links report), branded queries, and which formats earn links. Double down on formats that do; drop those that do not.

## Sequence

1. Ship and verify indexing (see `docs/SEO.md` checklists).
2. Publish one format weekly for a month; share once, in the right places.
3. Add the ranking-graphic export and the public ratings dataset.
4. Review at 30 days against the monitoring plan.
